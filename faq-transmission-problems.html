Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common signs of transmission problems?

Common warning signs are not shifting, slipping in certain gears, and noises. Vehicles also come in with delayed or rough shifting, a burning smell or dark fluid, grinding or shaking when changing gears, a check-engine or transmission warning light, red or brown fluid leaking under the vehicle, or a car that will not go into gear.

Is a red or brown fluid leak a transmission problem?

Red or brown fluid leaking under the vehicle is one of the signs the shop checks. Diagnosis is free unless the transmission must be removed and opened.

What does a burning smell from the transmission mean?

A burning smell, or transmission fluid that looks dark or burnt, is a reason to have the transmission checked. Diagnosis is free unless the transmission must be removed and opened.

Can a warning light mean transmission trouble?

A check-engine light or transmission warning light is a reason to have the transmission checked. Diagnosis is free unless the transmission must be removed and opened.

Why is my transmission slipping?

Slipping in certain gears is a common warning sign. It often feels like the engine revs and the vehicle does not accelerate as expected. Diagnosis is free unless the transmission must be removed and opened.

How long does a transmission rebuild take?

A rebuild usually takes 2 days or more.

Should I rebuild or replace my transmission?

It depends on how badly the transmission is damaged. A rebuild repairs the existing unit and replaces worn parts. Replacement swaps in a used or remanufactured unit. CVTs are usually replaced rather than rebuilt, and the shop recommends one after diagnosis.

What does California require before a shop repairs my transmission?

Based on the California Bureau of Automotive Repair rules, you get a written estimate for parts and labor and must authorize it before work begins. If the transmission has to be taken apart to find the problem, you get a written teardown estimate and approve it first. The invoice must identify any parts that are used, rebuilt or reconditioned. The source is the California Bureau of Automotive Repair: https://www.bar.ca.gov/consumer.
