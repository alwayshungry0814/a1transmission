Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common signs of transmission problems?

Drivers usually notice slipping gears, delayed engagement into Drive or Reverse, rough or harsh shifting, a burning smell, red or brown fluid under the car, grinding or shaking when changing gears, or a Check Engine / Transmission Warning light. Any one of these is worth checking early. Catching a fluid leak or solenoid issue before internal damage spreads can save time and keep you safer on SFV freeways and local streets.

Why is my transmission slipping?

Slipping feels like the engine revs without matching acceleration — as if the car is briefly in neutral. Low or burnt fluid, worn clutch packs, torque converter problems, and internal wear are common causes. Continuing to drive a slipping transmission often makes the repair more involved. Bring the vehicle to A1 Transmission for a free diagnostic and road test so we can pinpoint the issue rather than guess.

Should I keep driving with a slipping transmission?

Continuing to drive with active slipping, no engagement, or a strong burnt smell can worsen internal damage. If the vehicle will not move safely, overheats, or symptoms are severe, arrange a tow to a transmission shop rather than forcing the drive. For milder early signs (light delay, small leak, new warning light), schedule a free diagnostic soon so we can confirm the cause before it spreads. Call or text A1 Transmission at (818) 882-5135 for guidance on your symptoms.

What does delayed engagement mean?

Delayed engagement is a noticeable pause after you shift into Drive or Reverse before the vehicle actually moves. It can stem from low fluid, valve body concerns, worn seals, or clutch wear inside the transmission. Many Winnetka and San Fernando Valley drivers first notice it when backing out of a driveway or pulling into traffic. Early inspection often prevents a larger failure.

Is a red or brown fluid leak a transmission problem?

Yes. Automatic transmission fluid is typically red or reddish-brown when healthy and can darken as it ages. Leaks may come from the pan gasket, seals, cooler lines, or the case. Low fluid leads to overheating and accelerated wear. If you see spots under the middle or front of the car after parking at home or at work, schedule an inspection at our Saticoy Street shop before topping off fluid becomes a temporary habit.

What causes a burning smell from the transmission?

A burnt odor usually means the fluid has overheated and broken down. Dark, burnt fluid no longer lubricates or cools parts effectively. Heavy loads, low fluid from a leak, clogged cooler lines, or failing internal components can all contribute. If the smell is strong or your temperature warning appears, stop driving when safe and call (818) 882-5135 for help.

Can a Check Engine light mean transmission trouble?

Absolutely. Codes related to shift solenoids, sensors, gear ratios, or overheating often point to transmission or related control issues. A code scan is a starting point — not the full diagnosis. We combine computer data with a fluid check and road test so you get an accurate picture of whether the problem is transmission-related and what options you have.

Should I repair or rebuild my transmission?

A targeted repair can address a specific failed part — for example a solenoid, sensor, external leak, or cooler line. A rebuild means disassembling the unit, inspecting gears, clutch packs, seals, and bearings, and replacing worn components with quality parts. The right path depends on the diagnosis, vehicle mileage, and overall condition. We explain both options with a clear, itemized estimate before any work starts. Learn more on our transmission repair & rebuild service page.
