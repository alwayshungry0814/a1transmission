Quick Answer

What causes a rumble-strip vibration at 35–55 mph? Most often it is torque converter clutch (TCC) shudder, caused by worn lockup clutch material, degraded transmission fluid, or hydraulic pressure loss (sometimes accompanied by OBD-II code P0741). Catching it early can prevent metal debris from contaminating the rest of the transmission.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does torque converter shudder feel like?

It feels like driving over highway rumble strips during light acceleration between 35 and 55 mph, and often stops briefly when you lift off the gas or tap the brake.

Can a transmission fluid flush fix torque converter shudder?

If caught very early when degraded fluid is causing clutch chatter, a full fluid flush can sometimes resolve it. If the clutch lining is already worn or contaminating the fluid, torque converter replacement or a rebuild is needed.

What happens if you ignore torque converter shudder?

Ignoring it overheats the transmission fluid and circulates friction material and metal debris throughout the valve body and clutch packs, turning a targeted fix into a full rebuild.
